# DealLens

A dealer hands you a number. Is it good? The honest answer needs three things
you don't have in the showroom: where that number sits in the distribution of
comparable listings, what the price has done over the last two years and why,
and what you can defensibly say when you negotiate.

DealLens takes a quote — `/deal/honda/2022/civic?quote=24500` — and renders all
three. The verdict is server-rendered, so it works with JavaScript turned off.

## Trust as an engineering problem

The premise is that a pricing tool's real product is credibility, and
credibility is buildable. Three mechanisms carry it:

**Isomorphic conclusions.** The verdict is computed server-side and rendered as
HTML. D3 charts render as pure SVG on the server, then hydrate in place when
scrolled into view — the static skeleton upgrades with zero layout shift. The
URL is the only state mechanism, so there's no client store to desynchronize
and every view is shareable by construction.

**Typed data honesty.** The GraphQL schema carries a `DataSourceTag: REAL | DEMO`
enum on every source. NHTSA vPIC (vehicle specs) and EPA fueleconomy.gov (fuel
economy and weekly fuel prices) are live and typed `REAL`. The pricing dataset
is deterministic synthetic data, typed `DEMO`, and it renders a "Demo pricing
data" badge everywhere it appears. The point of putting this in the schema
rather than in a convention is that the UI _cannot_ forget to disclose it.

**Performance as a gate.** Lighthouse 100 across Performance, Accessibility,
Best Practices, and SEO on all three pages — enforced by a CI budget gate, not
measured once and screenshotted. LCP lands at 0.51–0.52s, CLS at 0, TBT at 0ms,
and first-load JS at 48–74 KB against a 150 KB budget.

## Grounding the AI: math decides, AI narrates

Every number the AI says traces back to a FACTS block computed server-side in a
pure, snapshot-tested module. Client-side numbers never enter a prompt.

- **Deal brief** — a streamed negotiation summary, permanently labelled
  "AI-generated · grounded in the numbers above".
- **Natural-language finder** — turns "reliable family SUV under $30k" into up
  to three candidates, constrained to a make whitelist and then verified against
  live vPIC. Hallucinated models are dropped after generation, and the UI reports
  how many survived.
- **Ask about this deal** — multi-turn Q&A that rebuilds the FACTS block per
  question, server-side, and treats user input strictly as a question rather
  than an instruction.

Cost control is part of the design, not an afterthought: per-IP and global daily
limits with honest 429 responses, a response cache bucketed by vehicle and quote,
and `MOCK_AI=1` for deterministic zero-cost CI runs.

## Refusing to guess

The rule that shaped the most code: **thin markets get an honest empty state, never
interpolation.** Roughly 5% of demo vehicles are deliberately given thin markets
so that "not enough data to say — and we won't guess" stays a reachable,
demonstrable state rather than a branch nobody exercises.

The same instinct runs through the edges. When vPIC and EPA disagree on a model
name and prefix matching fails, the fuel-cost bar is _hidden_ rather than
estimated. When the fuel feed is down, the fallback assumption is displayed as
an assumption.

## Tested like it matters

- **89 unit and contract tests** (Vitest) — contract tests lock behavior against
  real captured API payloads, so an upstream shape change fails locally instead
  of in production.
- **56 E2E runs** (Playwright) across Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit — plus a
  dedicated no-JavaScript project, because "works without JS" is a claim that
  needs a test, not an intention.
- **CI pipeline:** lint → typecheck → unit → build → E2E matrix → Lighthouse
  budget gate.

## What it deliberately doesn't claim

No free API exposes real transaction prices, so DealLens does not pretend to
know them. The pricing distribution is synthetic and labelled as such at every
appearance, and the data layer is built behind an adapter so a real source can
be swapped in without touching the domain logic. Everything downstream of that
— the percentile math, the verdict thresholds, the negotiation chips — is real
and would not change.
